## What it is

Waxing removes hair from the root, so skin stays smooth for longer than shaving. We offer two methods.

**Strip wax** is spread in a thin layer and removed with a cloth strip, a quick method for larger areas. **Hot wax** is applied a little thicker, sets on the skin and is lifted off without a strip. It is often chosen for delicate areas such as the lip, underarms and bikini line.

The menu covers eyebrows, lip and chin, underarms, arms, legs, bikini tidy and slim, intimate waxing and the back. Men's waxing, including chest and back, is on our page for men.

## Who it suits

Waxing suits most adults with hair they would like to remove for a longer period of time. Regular waxing can mean hair regrows finer for some people, though this varies.

Please tell us if you are pregnant, take medication such as retinoids or steroids, have sunburnt, broken or irritated skin, or have a skin condition such as eczema, or varicose veins in the area. We may suggest waiting, using a different method, or checking with your GP. Waxing should not be done over moles, warts, cuts or inflamed skin.

## What to expect

1. **Before your treatment.** Your therapist will ask about your skin, medication and any past reactions, and explain the method they recommend. They will clean the area. Your hair should be around 5 mm long. If you are unsure, ask us.
2. **During your treatment.** Wax is applied and removed in small sections, with a soothing finish afterwards. You may feel a brief sting. Tell your therapist if you are uncomfortable, and we will go at your pace.
3. **Afterwards.** Skin can look pink or slightly raised for a short time. This usually settles within a few hours.

## Aftercare

For the next 24 hours, avoid hot baths, saunas, swimming, sunbathing, sunbeds, heavy exercise, perfumed products and tight clothing on the waxed area. Do not scratch or pick at the skin.

After a day or two, gently exfoliate and moisturise to help prevent ingrown hairs. If you notice persistent redness, swelling, blistering or signs of infection, contact the salon or speak to your GP.
